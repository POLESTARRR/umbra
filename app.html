Burning is not your problem

·

The UV index measures sunburn. It was designed in Australia and Europe for skin that burns easily. If you tan rather than burn, it is quietly reporting the wrong thing, because tanning, dark spots, melasma and ageing are driven by UVA, which behaves completely differently.

Ozone destroys UVB when the sun is low, which is why sunburn risk falls off a cliff after four. It barely touches UVA, which is also why UVA comes through cloud, and through glass. Dashed line is sunburn; solid line is pigmentation.

How long can you stay out?

Minutes on bare skin · your row is marked

Time until unprotected skin has taken one full burn dose, at every index from 1 to 12. This is when damage is done. Visible redness shows up hours later.

Multiply by your sunscreen’s real protection factor to get your protected time. Read the bottle section first, because that number is almost never the one on the label.

A bottle is a shape, not a number

Tick what is on your label

SPF is measured in a lab under one fixed light, roughly midday sun. But when the sun is low, ozone has already stripped out the short UVB, so more of what still reaches you is UVA: 21% of your burn dose at noon, 72% by half five. Filters weighted to UVB fade at exactly the hours most people are actually outside.

So a sunscreen does not have one number. It has a shape across the day, and the useful question is not whether it is good but whether that shape fits your hours.
